## Starting position

Continue from [[Session 2 - The Jig of the North]]. John Guard is dead; all carts and horses were destroyed. The other travellers survived. Milo estimates about four days to the expedition. The expedition is looking for the tower and has not found it.

The cargo insurance, Melisor flask and expedition funding are known. The party believes they were intended victims; do not turn that belief into proof. The insurance covers cargo only.

The following scenes are proposed prep, not a record of events. Use the mountain crossing and waystation as this session's core; expedition arrival can close the session or open the next. Do not require every obstacle to become a fight.

## At the table

- **Opening question:** “What did you save from the wreck, and what are you doing with John's remains?” Let the players establish their answer.
- **Travel:** make one meaningful decision at the broken crossing. Use the wolves only if the group wants a fight and has recovered from the ambush.
- **Main conversation:** the toll keeper offers shelter and a practical problem; Wenzel offers a chance to pursue the ambush investigation.
- **Closing hook:** reach the search camp, report the caravan's destruction, and hear about the stolen supplies. If the waystation takes the evening, end there instead.

For a roughly three-hour session, allow about twenty minutes for the aftermath, forty-five for the crossing, an hour for the waystation, and the remaining time for camp arrival and closing decisions. These are pacing guides, not deadlines. Cut the wolf encounter before cutting a conversation the players care about.

### Four days on foot

Milo's estimate is about four days from the wreck, not four encounters. A suggested sequence is the crossing on the first travel day, the waystation on the second, then quieter travel before reaching camp on the fourth. This schedule is provisional until played; do not move established atlas locations to fit it.

At each overnight stop, note food, shelter, watches and any delay. Ask who travels with whom and give one pair a short conversation. Do not repeat six separate level-up scenes. Detours and long stops can change the arrival estimate; tell the party the expected cost before they choose one.

## Before leaving the wreck

Ask what the party actually recovered, who carries it, and whether they take anything to remember John. Record their answers rather than inventing a lost inventory. Give them time to recover before another fight. Milo wants the survivors to reach the expedition; he can repeat what he told them, not reveal the private conspiracy from the GM notes.

## Scene 1: Broken Mountain Crossing

**Map:** Session 3 — Broken Mountain Crossing. West entrance; east exit. The ravine runs north–south. The bridge's missing deck is about ten feet across at the scene scale. Loose rock lies beside the upper western approach.

**Opening:** The path ends at two wooden bridge sections hanging above the stream. Rope still joins the broken ends. On the western slope, fresh pale stone marks where the mountain has shed part of its face.

**Choices:** secure a rope crossing, brace the missing span with salvaged material, or find a longer route around. Tell players the span and drop before they commit. A visible lower ledge can provide a rescue position; establish the actual drop before resolving any fall. Do not spring an unannounced instant-death fall.

For an uncertain repair or route assessment, a suggested level-2 obstacle DC is 16 (Crafting or Survival as appropriate). A sound plan with adequate tools may need no check. Use the actual Leap, Long Jump, Climb and Grab an Edge rules for those actions, rather than replacing them with a generic group roll. A failed preparation check costs time or material; it does not automatically drop everybody into the ravine.

**Landslide option:** rocks have already blocked part of the western trail. Clear a narrow path together or take the visible detour. If you want an active slide, telegraph cracking stone and give time to withdraw; this prep does not include a damaging hazard statblock or automated rockfall.

**Optional wolves:** three published Monster Core Wolves, level 1 each, total encounter budget 90 XP (Low for six level-2 PCs; not 90 XP awarded to each character). Stage them in the western brush before the crossing or after everyone has reached safe ground. Growls and tracks provide warning. Food, deterrence or retreat can resolve the scene. Do not combine an attack with a split party hanging over the ravine. The wolves retreat when the prey is too dangerous; there are no automatic reinforcements.

Foundry: cliff and railing walls block ordinary token movement but not sight. The GM moves tokens across the broken span after resolving a successful crossing. Falls and terrain costs are manual.

## Scene 2: Mountain Toll Waystation

**Map:** Session 3 — Mountain Toll Waystation. The western building contains a kitchen, office and bunk room. The eastern shelter houses the visiting necromancer. The toll boom blocks movement but not sight.

**Opening:** A striped timber bars the road. Behind it, a keeper looks from the travellers' soot-stained clothes to the empty road behind them. “On foot? Where's the rest of your caravan?”

**Toll keeper:** working role, not a new named canon NPC. Wants the crossing recorded and the station maintained. Will not leave the post unattended or surrender the ledger. Start with questions, not a fight. Offer payment at a price you choose before play, useful maintenance work, or Milo's written undertaking. Do not manufacture a fixed toll or faction affiliation as established lore.

**Suggested exchange:** the keeper asks them to clear fallen stone from the station approach in return for passage and a dry place to rest. Milo can record the destroyed caravan in the ledger while they work. If they refuse the job, accept his undertaking rather than turning the toll into a mandatory combat. The keeper asks names, origin and destination; the ledger is a travel record, not a convenient confession of the insurance plot.

**Voice:** tired, practical, unimpressed by dramatic claims. “I can give you somewhere dry. I still need to know who came through.” The keeper can sympathize with John's death without accepting every accusation against the expedition.

**Wenzel Grauth:** use [[Wenzel Grauth]], not a new NPC called Wendell. His presence at the station is the proposed scene hook. He wants to leave the frontier and will run if confronted. His existing actor and portrait are already in the Saeroth module; do not create a duplicate.

### Bringing Wenzel into the conversation

Let the party see him before they know his role: a traveller with his bag already packed, listening from the eastern shelter. When Milo describes the burning wagons, Wenzel stops what he is doing and asks which road they used. Give that observable reaction freely; it is a reason to ask questions, not proof of guilt.

If approached casually, he calls himself a practitioner heading away from the frontier. If accused of being a Lichdom agent, he immediately corrects that claim. Show the recovered flask only if the party actually kept it; otherwise a description of the trap can start the conversation.

Once his involvement becomes clear, his priority is escape. If cornered and offered a credible way to survive, he can trade what he knows. Play his fear of consequences, not remorse that erases his part in John's death. A useful opening line is: “I was paid for the work. I wasn't told who you were.”

### What Wenzel knows

- He prepared the undead and strapped supplied alchemist's fire into their bodies.
- He was told the wagons were a Melisor consignment and nobody aboard mattered.
- He was supplied twenty-four lesser alchemist's fire flasks; he did not manufacture them.
- He can describe the packing crate and its maker's mark.
- He was instructed to leave a Melisor reagent flask and did not understand why.
- He can identify the intermediary who hired him. He has never met [[Cassian Merovel]] and cannot give the party that name.
- He was not told about the insurance. Do not let him explain the whole fraud.

**Ways forward:** question him quietly, confront him with the flask, follow him when he leaves, or promise safe passage in exchange for testimony. Basic visible evidence and his accessible possessions should not all sit behind a single roll. If the players let him leave, preserve a later lead through his departure direction or the keeper's ordinary recollection; do not force capture.

**If it turns violent:** use his existing level-3 sheet. He tries to escape, not fight to the death. The toll keeper protects the station and does not automatically join either side. Do not add undeclared undead reinforcements. Decide the intermediary's identity before providing an actual name; it is not supplied by this prep.

### Investigation outcomes

- **They bargain:** Wenzel's account establishes his involvement and provides a description of the supplier's crate and intermediary. Record his exact claims so they can be checked later. Testimony is not independent proof of the whole conspiracy.
- **They capture him:** ask who guards him and whether they intend to bring him to camp. Milo wants the group moving; the keeper will not become a permanent jailer. Do not make an off-screen escape automatic.
- **He escapes or they ignore him:** continue toward the expedition. The keeper can report the direction they actually saw him take. Do not plant a replacement confession in his room.
- **They kill him:** preserve only evidence and statements already established. The investigation can continue through the flask, cargo records and expedition funding without speaking to him.

The useful revelation is that someone supplied and directed the attack. Wenzel's statement that nobody aboard mattered supports the party's fear, but it does not establish an explicit order to kill them. His ignorance of the insurance and Cassian keeps those questions open.

## Scene 3: Expedition Search Camp

**Map:** Session 3 — Expedition Search Camp. Southern arrival trail, central fire, southwest supply crates, southeast command pavilion, northward scouting route. No tower is visible or located here.

**Opening:** Canvas tents fill a sheltered patch of mountain ground. Survey stakes and muddy boots stand outside the command pavilion. Someone stops counting crates long enough to ask, “Where are the wagons?”

The leader wants a usable account of the attack and enough supplies to keep the search parties in the mountains. Their name and personal history remain open. They are not automatically a conspirator because Melisor funds the expedition.

Ask the party for witnesses, recovered evidence and an account of the losses. The search has not found the tower. Maps show surveyed routes and remaining leads, not an exact tower location. Let the party's suspicions shape the conversation without making everyone hostile.

**Suggested leader approach:** offer a place to put down their packs before asking for the report. Hear Milo and the party together, then separate direct observations from conclusions. “Tell me what you saw first. Then tell me what you think it means.” Funding by Melisor is not a secret to deny; knowledge of the ambush is not something the leader automatically possesses.

Let the party examine the search map. It should show checked routes and unresolved leads, with no convenient tower marker. If they want to investigate the expedition before accepting work, allow conversations with staff and inspection of ordinary supplies; do not manufacture incriminating records merely because they expect to find them.

### Recovery job for the following session

Orc bandits have taken resources the expedition needs. Suggested cargo is food and climbing equipment. For this hook, the leader cannot spare the remaining camp staff while keeping search teams supplied.

**Offer:** “We've lost supplies to bandits as well. Bring back what you can, and we can keep the search going. I need the goods, not a pile of heads.” Before they agree, identify the missing cargo, mark its last known location, and agree on payment. Suggested non-cash terms are meals and a place in camp while they assist the search, plus access to its survey information; choose any coin reward during the next encounter's treasure prep rather than promising an unspecified fortune.

**Questions to answer:** how were the thieves identified, who last saw the cargo, and how will the recovered goods be recognized? Establish these before sending the party out. A witness and an inventory are enough; the leader does not need to know the bandits' entire camp layout.

Recovering the goods is the objective. Negotiation, an exchange, stealth or combat are all valid. Leave the bandits' numbers, location, motives and reward for the next encounter prep; no balanced bandit fight is claimed ready here. Keep the theft separate from the caravan conspiracy unless you deliberately connect them.

**End beat:** the leader unfolds a survey map and points out the last known location of the stolen supplies. The tower remains somewhere beyond the mapped routes.

If the party declines, the leader asks their intended next step. They can investigate the attack, help a survey team, or pursue their own route. The expedition continues searching; accepting the recovery job is not the only way to participate.

## Before play and after play

Before play, choose the bridge's actual drop and safe rescue position, select actors for any optional fight, and decide whether the expedition leader needs a name this session. If Wenzel may identify his employer, prepare that intermediary separately without substituting Cassian. Keep the orc recovery fight for the next prep unless it has been fully built and checked.

After play, record the elapsed travel days, remaining food and recovered cargo, any arrangements for John's remains, Wenzel's fate and statements, and the party's current location. Note whether they accepted the recovery job and its agreed terms. Move only events that actually happened into the session record; retain unused scenes as prep.

Tents in the search camp are exterior scenery, not furnished playable interiors. The command pavilion is accessible through its open flap.

No combat, weather damage, falls or scene activation is automated. Place party tokens at the entrance when ready; do not move existing campaign tokens or alter their HP. For the optional wolf scene, import three tokens from the published Wolf actor. Place the existing Wenzel actor inside the eastern waystation shelter. Toll keeper and leader remain roleplay positions until you choose actors for them.
