**Sigillum Dei** is the title a High Prince's witnesses carry. He names them at
his crowning and they hold it until they die.

Who can be named has not moved in two hundred years. Every direct vassal of the
Crown, each bringing three household knights, and the prince's own retinue on
top. That is the five territorial electors and the thirty-odd nobles who hold
of the Crown rather than of an elector, so the body comes to something near a
hundred and seventy under arms.

What changed is the road. Before 2316 the witnesses were summoned to Reichsmund
and a Thesal celebrant came down the Pilgrim's Stair to do the crowning. Now
the column walks to Concord instead. It goes up the Stair and takes about fifty
days. A Vaelic interregnum is measured in seasons.

## The early snow

In 2305 the snow shut the pass early and the Diet deadlocked three against
three. The faction holding Reichsmund found a celebrant who had wintered on the
wrong side of the mountain, and crowned Gerhard Dravensk with him. Eleven years
of war came out of that. The [[The Pilgrim's Peace|Peace]] was signed at
Concord in 2316, and the crowning moved there with it. A prince crowned in
Concord cannot be crowned by whoever a faction managed to find. See
[[The Two-Crown War]].

## The column

The [[Drake Rider Order]] clears the Stair ahead of it and holds both ends
until the last cart is over. No charter obliges them to. Grooms, smiths,
farriers and baggage go on top of the hundred and seventy, and the whole thing
has to be fed over a mountain.

A place in the column cannot be won by fighting, which makes it the one
distinction in [[Vaelic Principality|Vaelic]] that birth and favour decide
outright. The competition is vicious. A house that has spent a generation
arguing it holds directly of the Crown, rather than of some elector, will make
that argument loudest in the year a prince is dying.

## The seal

At the crowning the celebrant sets a seal of gold and crimson wax on the breast
of each man's armour. It holds down a prayer of protection, written on
parchment worked for the purpose. Everyone named takes one, vassal and
household knight alike. The title is held for life and does not pass to a son.

Wax breaks. A hard blow to the chest costs a man the prayer and the seal in the
same stroke, and no priest in Vaelic can set another. Only Concord can. So the
honour arrives with a standing reason to walk the road again, which the Council
of Hospices has never once described as deliberate.

- **What the prayer does** Nobody agrees, because nobody wearing one is keen to
  test it. The Council calls it a protection and declines to be more specific
- **Broken seals** Worn anyway, by most. A man who rode to Concord and came back
  with the wax cracked has still ridden to Concord, and saying otherwise in
  company is a way to end an evening

## The second crown

Aleksander Kesslin named witnesses at Ostravin in 2305, the same as any prince,
and the Cathedral Court of [[Dalstan]] sealed them. Vaelic's regnal list skips
his name. It follows that it skips theirs, and the Crown has never issued a
finding either way. The last of those men died some years ago. Their houses
have not dropped the matter, and [[House Kesslin]] least of all.
