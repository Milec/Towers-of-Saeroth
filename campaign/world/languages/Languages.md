Almost everybody speaks Common. A dockhand in Vessene and a herder on the
Uplad can do business without either of them working at it, and neither of
them thinks that is remarkable, which is the part worth noticing. No empire
imposed it. It was already there when the nations resettled the world, in the
same way the calendar was.

Most peoples keep a second language of their own beside it. It is spoken at
home, at funerals, and over a bargain somebody does not want overheard, and
rarely in front of strangers. A [[Sahenna Compact]] clan will conduct an
entire trade in Common and then argue about it among themselves in something
else while the buyer stands there.

## Draconic

Serious scholarship is written in **Draconic**, and no living institution can
explain why. There are very few dragons in Saeroth and none of them are
consulted. The answer everyone gives is that it was already the language
every worthwhile treatise was written in by the time anyone thought to ask.

- [[Melisor Magocracy]] examines in it, and an academy will fail a candidate
  whose Draconic is merely serviceable.
- [[Xian Ti]]'s ministries cite it, translate it into the imperial hand for
  the record, and keep the Draconic original filed beneath.
- A physician in [[Sarrowmere]] writes the diagnosis in Common and the dose
  in Draconic, which the guilds insist is for precision and is also very
  good for business.

Anyone with a real education reads it. Nobody outside one speaks it.

## At the table

- **Assume Common and move on.** A conversation only becomes a language
  problem when you decide it should be one, and it lands much harder if it has
  not happened for six sessions.
- **A second language is a door into a family**, not a puzzle. The interesting
  thing about a character who speaks Sahenna at home is what gets said in
  front of them.
- **Draconic marks a person as schooled**, and in most of the world that is a
  class marker rather than a magical one. A mercenary who reads it has a story
  they may not want told.
