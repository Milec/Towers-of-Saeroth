Six years ago [[Thurigypt]] put an army across the ocean into the
[[Lazarian Lichdom]]'s delta, and had it back — what there was of it — before
the next flood.

This is the war the phrase *no major war in sixty years* has to be careful
about. It was a real one: a great power fully committed, its whole fleet, its
household regiments, a Pharaoh in the field. It simply did not last, and it
did not spread one inch beyond the two nations fighting it. Everything
interesting about it is in that second fact.

## Why the Pharaoh went

Because [[Lazarus]] had not spoken in five years.

The Lich Emperor's silence was eleven years old this season and was already
five when the fleet sailed. Thurigypt's priest-kings read it the way most of
the world read it: that the thing on the throne at Grauthaven was finished,
that the Cathedral Court was governing an empty chair, and that a state held
together by one immortal will could be broken while that will was absent.

Pharaoh **Amenkhet** had a further reason, and it was a good one. Thurigypt's
patron is [[Pharasma]], whom Lazarian has outlawed outright, and the delta
holds the graves of every expedition Thurigypt has sent against it for four
centuries. This was not a war of conquest. It was framed, sincerely, as a
recovery of the dead.

## Why nobody joined

Not one nation came in on either side, and it was not squeamishness.

- [[Thurion Merchant Alliance]] declined the credit. A campaign against a
  non-signatory is not forbidden by the [[The Pilgrim's Peace|Pilgrim's Peace]], but the Alliance's
  committee read the risk correctly and would not fund a landing on a coast
  nobody has ever held.
- [[Aquoniti]] would not carry troops at any price offered. Thurigypt sailed in
  its own bottoms, which is the reason the whole thing had to be done in one
  season.
- The Muster Court at [[Kelvary March]] refused every charter, on a principle
  the March states plainly and has never bent: Kelvary does not sell regiments
  to fight the Lichdom, because Kelvary's dead do not stay sold.
- [[Thesal Theocracy]] would not bless it and would not condemn it, and sent
  hospital corps to the Thurigypt staging ports instead, where they were
  extremely busy.

So Amenkhet fought it exactly as the Peace's architects intended a war to have
to be fought: out of his own granaries, in his own ships, with his own people,
and with no second season available at any price. The Peace did not forbid the
Delta War. It priced it, and the price was the whole thing.

## What happened

The landing worked. The first three weeks worked. Thurigypt is very good at
this and the delta's living population is small.

Then the ground fought back, in the sense that the ground is what the Lichdom
grows its armies out of. Every Thurigypt soldier who fell in the marsh was
standing up again within a day, on the other side, knowing the formation drill
and the passwords and the faces. The Cathedral Court did not need to raise
levies. It needed only to let the campaign continue.

By the fourth month the Pharaoh was fighting his own household regiments.

He withdrew what he could — perhaps a third — and it is to his credit that he
went last and got that third home, which is the only thing about the Delta War
that Thurigypt's court can say out loud without an argument starting.

## What it proved, and what it cost

- **Lazarus's absence changes nothing.** The Lichdom defended itself without an
  edict, without a mobilisation, and apparently without hurry. Whatever the
  silence at Grauthaven means, six years ago the last nation to bet on it being
  weakness lost an army finding out. Every court on the board has revised its
  estimate accordingly, and nobody now says *the Lich Emperor is gone* in a
  room where it might be repeated.
- **Thurigypt has not recovered.** A generation of young men is in the marsh,
  standing. The treasury went into the ships. The flood has come in short two
  years running, and a Pharaoh who had spent nothing would still be in trouble
  over that — a Pharaoh who spent everything is in a different kind of trouble,
  which is what the priest-kings are actually arguing about when they argue
  about engineering.
- **The Peace held without being invoked.** No clause was triggered and no
  arbitration was called. The war stayed small because the supply of other
  people's money, ships, soldiers and grain had already been withdrawn, sixty
  years earlier, in advance, from everybody. This is the case study every
  signatory cites, and it is the reason nobody has seriously tested the treaty
  since.
- **And the world has decided it does not want another.** Six years is not
  long. There are men in every port who were pulled out of that marsh, and
  their account of what it is like to be shot at by a friend has done more for
  the Pilgrim's Peace than sixty years of Concord Table minutes.

## Threads

- **The recovered dead.** Thurigypt's stated war aim was to bring its dead
  home, and it came back with fewer than it went with. Pharasma's temples have
  been funding smaller and quieter attempts ever since — not fleets, expeditions
  — and one of them is always hiring.
- **Somebody sold the Pharaoh the reading.** The intelligence that Lazarus was
  finished came from somewhere, was very confident, and was wrong in a way that
  cost Thurigypt everything. The [[Undertide Reaches]] sell that sort of
  assessment. So do several people in [[Quivar]]. Nobody has ever traced it.
- **The Cathedral Court's minutes.** The Lichdom's own record of the year the
  Delta War was fought reads as an ordinary administrative year. Either the
  Court did not consider an invasion worth minuting, or somebody removed it,
  and both readings are worse than the other depending on the hour.
