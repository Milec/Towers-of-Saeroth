Between the year the rain came down red and the first settlement that leaves
anything to dig up, there is about a century in which nothing was built,
nothing was farmed on a scale worth the name, and nothing whatever was written
down.

Scholars call it the Silent Years, and the name is not poetic. It is a
description of the evidence.

## Every archive stops in the same place

[[Xian Ti]]'s ministries have added a document a year since the first
examination, and the series begins after the gap. The [[Elven Confederacy]]'s tribal memory
is continuous from the moment it starts, and it starts after the gap. The
[[Sahenna Compact]]'s Storytellers recite a line of names that simply has no
earlier end. The [[Stoneborn Holds]] carve their dates into the rock, and the
oldest carved date anyone has found is around 310.

Four traditions, four methods, no contact between them for centuries, and the
same hole in every one. A hole in one archive is a fire or a flood. Four of
them is a fact about the world, and it is the finding that keeps anybody
interested in a century where nothing happened.

The traditions with the most to lose by admitting it are the ones that admit
it most readily. Xian Ti's ministry files the gap plainly and has for eighteen
centuries. It is the seminar rooms in [[Melisor Magocracy]] that argue.

## The first tallies

What people did do in those years was count. Survivors began tallying from the
sky, separately, on both continents, and every calendar in use today descends
from one of those tallies. They agree to within a year or two. Not one of them
records what happened in year one, and every tradition that keeps a count
inherited a running total from somebody who was already counting.

Almost nobody finds this strange, because everybody grew up inside it. Ask a
factor in Vessene
what AR stands for and he will tell you *After the Red* without pausing. Ask
him what the Red was and he will tell you the rain. Ask what caused the rain
and the conversation ends.

## The theories

The theories are old and none of them can be tested. A sickness in the upper
air. A sea that came up and went back down. A mountain that came apart. A war
among things that do not live on the ground, which is the one the temples
prefer and the one the academies will not sign.

Melisor keeps a standing seminar on it and has narrowed nothing in two
hundred years. Xian Ti filed the whole question under mineralogy four
centuries ago, and a clerk wrote a question in the margin that has never been
answered and has never been removed either.

## The erasure

**GM.** The gap is the erasure. See [[The Nameless Empire]] and
[[The God Sundering]].

Two continents were emptied in the space of seven days, and the people who
walked back into them were the ones the empire had barely administered and
therefore barely bothered to erase — herders, islanders, the edges of things.
They inherited no books, no ruins they could date, and no rumour. That was the
point of doing it that way.

So the Silent Years are not a period of decline. They are what a hundred years
looks like when everyone alive is a survivor with no institution behind them,
and it takes that long for one of them to build something that lasts.

## Threads

- **A dig that goes one layer too deep.** Below the resettlement layer is the
  red band, and below that, in a few places, is worked stone. Anyone who finds
  it has found something the erasure missed, and the towers have lately made
  such finds worth money to people who never cared before.
- **The clerk's marginal question.** It is still in the Xian Ti archive, in
  the hand of a man whose name is on the file. A party with a reason to be in
  that building could read it.
- **Somebody's calendar disagrees.** A tradition whose count is off by more
  than two years would be either a mistake or the only independent evidence
  anybody has. Both are worth a journey to check.
