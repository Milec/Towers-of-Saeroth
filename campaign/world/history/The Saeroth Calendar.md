The calendar used on Concord Table records, caravan ledgers, and most maps is
the **Concord standard**. It does not replace local calendars. A Thurigyptian
scribe may still date a flood by the river, an elf by a remembered season, and
a Stoneborn contract by a carved count. When people from different places need
one date, they use this one.

It keeps the shared **AR** count — *After the Red* — but its month names were
standardized after [[The Pilgrim's Peace|the Pilgrim's Peace]]. The almanac
clerks at Concord chose words that could be recognized across the continent:
the rain, the empty years, the settlements, the roads, the Concord, the war,
and the peace. They did not claim to know what caused the Red. Nobody does.

## The year

An ordinary year has **343 days**: twelve months of 28 days, followed by the
seven **Red Days**. Every month has exactly four seven-day weeks. Red Days are
not a thirteenth month; together they make the last full week of the year.
There are no leap days in the common count.

The calendar is useful precisely because its arithmetic is dull. A date on the
same numbered day of any month is always the same day of the week. Caravans
can plan a four-week leg, a quarter, or a full year without consulting a local
priest or a court astronomer.

## Months

| Order | Month | What the name remembers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Redwake** | The world waking under the red rain remembered in every origin story. |
| 2 | **Stillwater** | The floods and the silence after the Red. |
| 3 | **Hearthrise** | The first permanent settlements of the [[The Resettlement]]. |
| 4 | **Rootmeet** | The early pacts, holds, and lineages that endured after resettlement. |
| 5 | **Charter** | The old kingdoms and compacts that made promises into institutions. |
| 6 | **Highroad** | The roads, wells, and trade routes that connected them. |
| 7 | **Concord** | The centuries when one church-state held its three orders together. |
| 8 | **Crownfall** | The cost of divided crowns and the [[The Two-Crown War]]. |
| 9 | **Peacebond** | The promises and limits written in the Pilgrim's Peace. |
| 10 | **Emberturn** | The red layer beneath every field and the work of rebuilding on dangerous ground. |
| 11 | **Longwatch** | The borders, patrols, and old disputes that did not end when the war did. |
| 12 | **Starwake** | The traveller's final month, when routes are checked before a new year's road. |

Names are capitalized in records but often shortened in speech: *the 18th of
Road*, *last Concord*, or *before Peacebond*. Regional almanacs may put local
seasonal names beside these terms; contracts use the Concord names alone.

## Days of the week

The week follows the folk memory of what people say came after the sky turned
red: rain, flood, a household, a road, an agreement, a watch, and then the
silence. The sequence is civic rather than religious, which is why it travels
well between nations.

| Order | Day | Common use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Redfall** | New accounts, departures, and the beginning of a work week. |
| 2 | **Floodtide** | Water and cargo work; a common day for river and harbour schedules. |
| 3 | **Hearthday** | Household markets, repairs, and family obligations. |
| 4 | **Roadmark** | Caravans commonly change watches or make planned stops. |
| 5 | **Oathday** | Courts, guild meetings, and contracts; a traditional but not mandatory custom. |
| 6 | **Watchday** | Muster, patrol, and public notices in many towns. |
| 7 | **Stillday** | Rest, worship, and remembrance, observed differently by each faith. |

## The Red Days

After Starwake's twenty-eighth day come the **Seven Red Days**: Redfall through
Stillday one final time. They are kept as a whole week, not numbered as a
month. Many towns close courts on Stillday; some faiths hold vigils; others
simply settle old ledgers before Redwake begins again. The shared custom is
restraint, not a single rite.

## Writing dates

Formal records use the weekday, day number, month, and AR year:

> **Oathday, 12 Peacebond, 2376 AR**

During the year-end observance, name the day within Red Week instead:

> **Roadmark of Red Week, 2376 AR**

For a GM, use the Concord standard whenever a travel leg, deadline, or dated
event needs a clear answer. It deliberately leaves regional ceremonies and
seasonal weather to the country where play is happening.
