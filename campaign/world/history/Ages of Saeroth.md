## The Long Peace

The sixty years since the Pilgrim's Peace are the longest stretch without a
general war in anything the archives can reach, and the world is aware of it in
the way a man is aware of good weather. Every nation dates its modern politics
from the Peace. Most of them teach the four clauses in schools.

**What "no major war" actually means.** The Peace defines a war as a declared
campaign between signatory states, and there has not been one in sixty years.
That is a real claim, not a lawyer's one — but it leaves a great deal of
violence outside itself, all of which is ordinary and none of which anyone
calls a war:

- **Raiding and skirmish**, most years, on half a dozen frontiers. Homesteads
  burned on the Kesmarch line, herds driven off the steppe edge, logging camps
  in the northern pine that wake up surrounded.
- **Patrol actions** across borders nobody has ever surveyed, fought by
  marshals and militia and written up as police work by both sides.
- **The oldest frontier in the world**, between the Khaganate and Xian Ti,
  which has been continuously garrisoned for six centuries and continuously
  shot at, and which no signatory considers a war because neither party ever
  signed anything.
- **The Delta War**, six years ago, which was unambiguously a major war and
  which the Peace did not forbid, contain by arbitration, or need to — see the
  note. It is the exception everyone cites, and it lasted one season precisely
  because of the treaty.

The distinction is not pedantry and the people living on those frontiers do not
find it comforting. It is simply true that a caravan can cross the western
continent this season without an escort, and that this has been true for sixty
years, and that no adult alive has organised their life around anything else.

## What is ending it

[[The Towers]], and nothing else on the board.

Not because a tower is an army. Because the Peace is a machine for making war
unaffordable, and a conquered tower is understood — by everyone, on no evidence
whatsoever — to make its master answerable to no ledger at all. A world held
together by who can pay for what has been handed a prize that promises to end
the need to pay for anything.

The Concord Table has sat twice since the towers began and adjourned twice
without a finding.
