## Counting the years

Saeroth counts in **AR** — *After the Red* — and the present year is
**2376 AR**. To convert anything the notes say in plain speech, subtract:
sixty years ago is 2316 AR, four centuries ago is around 1976.

The remarkable thing about the count is not its length. It is that
**everybody's agrees**, to within a year or two, and **nobody can say what it
counts from**.

Xian Ti's ministries hold the longest unbroken series — they have added one
every year since the first examination and inherited a number when they began.
The elven tribes keep a parallel count from tribal memory. Thurion's commercial
code dates contracts by it because a contract has to agree with the contract it
replaces. The Stoneborn carve it. The Sahenna Storytellers recite it. Every one
of these traditions arrived at the same number by a different route, and not
one of them has a founding event at year 1 — they simply inherited a running
total from people who were already counting.

Scholars have noticed. [[Melisor Magocracy]] has a standing seminar on it and
[[Xian Ti]] has a four-hundred-year marginal note asking the same question.
The usual answer is that the count must begin at some agreed astronomical
event nobody bothered to record, which satisfies nobody and is repeated
because the alternative is admitting the world does not know what year it is
counting from.

**GM:** it counts from the seven days the sky bled. The people who repopulated
an emptied world were scattered survivors the empire had barely administered
and therefore barely erased, and every one of those isolated groups started a
count from the most important thing that had ever happened to anybody. Their
counts agree because they are all counting the same sky. Every dated document
in Saeroth is reckoned from the murder of a god, and this is not knowable —
see [[The God Sundering]].

## The small wars

The [[The Pilgrim's Peace|Pilgrim's Peace]] defines a war as a declared
campaign between signatory states, and there has not been one since 2316. That
is a real claim rather than a lawyer's one, and it still leaves a great deal of
violence outside itself, none of which anybody calls a war:

- **Raiding and skirmish**, most years, on half a dozen frontiers. Homesteads
  burned on the Kesmarch line, herds driven off the steppe edge, logging camps
  in the northern pine that wake up surrounded.
- **Patrol actions** across borders nobody ever surveyed, fought by marshals
  and militia and written up as police work at both ends.
- **The frontier at 1776**, garrisoned and shot at for six centuries by two
  powers who never signed anything.
- **The Delta War of 2370**, which was unambiguously a major war and which the
  Peace did not forbid, contain by arbitration, or need to.

The distinction is not pedantry and the people on those frontiers do not find
it comforting. It is simply true that a caravan has crossed this continent
without an escort for sixty years, and that no adult alive has organised their
life around anything else.

## The towers

[[The Towers]], and nothing else on the board. Not because a tower is an army —
because the Peace is a machine for making war unaffordable, and a conquered
tower is understood, on no evidence whatever, to make its master answerable to
no ledger at all.

The Concord Table has sat twice since 2375 and adjourned twice without a
finding.
