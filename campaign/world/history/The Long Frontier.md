In 1776 the [[Khazan Khaganate]] and [[Xian Ti]] fortified the same line, from
opposite sides, within about four years of each other. Neither has taken it and
neither has left it. It has been garrisoned and shot at every year since, and
the ministry that files the casualty rolls has never missed a season.

The west measures its history against one catastrophic war that ended. The east
measures its against a war that did not, and the difference is not that the
easterners are more warlike. It is that both of these nations found a use for
the wall, and a war nobody needs to win is a war nobody has to stop.

## The proving ground

The Great Khagan is elected by the assembled khans and has been since 740. That
is the Khaganate before it is anything else, and an election needs candidates
with reputations.

There is exactly one place to earn one. A khan who has held the southern
pastures for nine years and lost no herds has a record; a khan who has ridden
the wall and come back with remounts, prisoners and a story the camps repeat
has a career. So the frontier is where Khazan makes the men who will one day
choose a Khagan, and every generation of khans has an interest in the wall
still being there when their sons are old enough to ride at it.

**Nobody says this out loud and nobody needs to.** The Khagan who took the line
outright would have handed the next election to whoever held the ground
afterwards, and would have taken away the only proving ground the Khaganate
has.

## The budget line

Xian Ti governs by examination, and a frontier command is the posting that
makes or ends a career in the imperial service. It costs the empire more than
anything else the empire does, it puts more men under one seal than any other
posting, and the casualty rolls run unbroken from 1776 to this spring — which
is the part the ministry is proud of, and says a good deal about the ministry.

An empire of clerks is an empire that must be able to point at something the
clerks are for. The frontier is that thing. A ministry that ended it would be
a ministry arguing for its own budget the following spring with nothing in
hand.

## The rules nobody admits to

Six hundred years of the same two armies produces conventions, and both courts
would deny that any exist.

- **The season is short.** Riding is done between the thaw and the first hard
  grazing, and a raid outside it is treated on both sides as something other
  than war, which is worse for whoever did it.
- **Officers are ransomed and clerks are not touched**, because a clerk is
  worth nothing and a burned record office costs both sides a generation of
  arguing about where the line actually runs.
- **The wall has gates and they are used.** Iron and horses cross it in
  quantity in years the ministry describes as quiet. Khazan's iron-rich hill
  country and Xian Ti's foundries are two halves of one trade that neither
  nation's official history mentions.
- **Nobody moves the line.** Ground changes hands, sometimes for decades, and
  it comes back. The last serious attempt to take the whole frontier was in
  2041, and the man who ordered it is remembered in both countries as a fool
  by two entirely different arguments.

## The west's treaty made no difference at all

When the [[The Pilgrim's Peace]] was drafted, the two frontier powers answered
it in opposite ways and nothing on the wall changed for either answer.

Xian Ti acceded by letter, the way it accedes to most things, and filed the
clauses. The Khagan declined outright, on the grounds that he does not sign
what he does not intend to keep — which the signatories found insulting at the
time and have had sixty years to reconsider.

Both positions were honest. Neither was about the frontier, because no clause
in that treaty covers a war that has been running since before the Concord
split, that neither side is trying to win, and that no bank finances.

## The rest of the continent

The frontier is not the east's only history, and it is the reason the rest of
it is quiet.

The Khaganate's ambition points north at a wall rather than west at
[[Kesmarch Frontier]]'s grazing or south at [[Qeshara Sultanate]]'s wells.
Xian Ti's army is committed to a line it cannot leave. The two powers that
could conquer the eastern continent have each been fully occupied by the other
for six centuries, which has left everybody else to get on with the
[[Economy|Salt Road]] and the harvest.

The grazing quarrel with Kesmarch has been before the Concord Table eight
times and settled none of them, and it stays a quarrel because the Khaganate's
serious riders are elsewhere every spring.

## At the table

- **Do not run it as a stalemate.** People die on that line every year and the
  families know exactly which year. It is not theatre and the men holding it
  do not think it is pointless.
- **The two tensions are the same tension.** The Great Khagan is old and has
  named no favourite, and Xian Ti's examination scandal has reached high enough
  that investigating it would discredit the ministry doing the investigating.
  A disputed succession means khans who need reputations, and a discredited
  ministry means a frontier budget somebody will finally question. Both point
  at the wall in the same season.
- **A tower on that line ends six hundred years of arrangement**, and neither
  court has a procedure for it. See [[The Towers]].
