The treaty that ended the [[The Two-Crown War|Two-Crown War]], named for the mountain pass whose
closing started it. Sixty years old, never once broken by a signatory, and the
reason a traveller can cross the western continent this season without seeing a
soldier who is not bored.

It is also not a peace treaty in the ordinary sense. It settled almost nothing
about who had been right. It was written by people who had watched a
succession quarrel eat a continent and who had concluded — correctly — that
the quarrel was never the dangerous part.

## The four clauses

Short enough to be recited, which was deliberate.

1. **No war may be begun over a succession, an election or an anointing.** A
   disputed crown goes to arbitration at Concord. The ruling is not binding on
   anybody.
2. **No signatory will lend to, insure, charter for, victual or carry a war of
   aggression between signatories.** This is the clause with teeth, and it is
   the only one that has ever had to be used.
3. **Border and claim disputes go before the Concord Table.** Its findings are
   likewise not binding. Refusing to appear is what triggers clause 2 against
   you, and that is the entire mechanism.
4. **The roads of pilgrimage stay open, and unfortified, in every season.**
   Written for the Pilgrim's Stair specifically. It is why the
   [[Vaelic Principality]] pays to keep that pass cleared to this day, and why
   the crossroads of [[Thesal Theocracy]] have no fortifications on them.

## Why it works

Nobody became better. What changed is who put their name to clause 2.

A war between great powers is not fought out of a treasury. It is fought on
[[Thurion Merchant Alliance]] credit, with [[Kelvary March]] regiments,
[[Tal Ulad]] remounts, [[Silicar]] powder and [[Melisor Magocracy]] artillery,
carried in [[Aquoniti]] bottoms and fed on somebody else's grain. Every one of
those parties came out of the Two-Crown War having lost catastrophically by
supplying it — Thurion nearly failed as a nation, Kelvary buried two companies
of its own neighbours at Ambry Ford, and the Hungry Year killed farmers in
countries that never raised a soldier.

So the treaty does not ask crowns to stop wanting things. It asks the
merchants, the bankers, the mercenaries, the mages and the shipmasters to
refuse the work, and every one of them had already decided to.

The result is that a modern aggressor faces something no aggressor faced
seventy years ago: **a campaign it must pay for in coin, out of its own
granaries, with its own people, and no second season.** Nobody on the board can
afford that. Not one.

## The Concord Table

The standing congress, meeting every third spring at Concord, where the world's
border quarrels are aired in front of everyone. [[Thesal Theocracy]] provides
the room, the record and no opinion whatsoever.

It has heard the same handful of disputes for decades and settled almost none
of them. That is not the failure it looks like. The Table's function is not
resolution — it is *attendance*: while you are arguing at Concord you are not
refusing to argue, and while you are not refusing, your credit is good. A
quarrel parked at the Table for thirty years is a quarrel that has not killed
anybody for thirty years.

Perennial items include the Kesmarch homestead charters, the Stoneborn and
Undertide claims on the same mountain from above and below, the logging in the
northern pine, and — new this cycle — Silicar's canal across the Ulad spring
route.

## What it does not cover

The gaps are as important as the clauses, and every one of them is somebody's
current problem.

- **Raiding, skirmish and patrol.** The Peace forbids *war*, which it defines
  as a declared campaign between signatory states. A burned homestead, a
  driven-off herd, a marshal's patrol shot at across a line nobody surveyed:
  none of that is a war, all of it happens most years, and everyone involved
  is careful to keep it that way.
- **Non-signatories.** The [[Lazarian Lichdom]] was never invited and is bound
  by nothing. The [[Khazan Khaganate]] declined outright, on the grounds that
  the Khagan does not sign what he does not intend to keep — which the
  signatories found insulting at the time and have come to regard as the most
  honest answer anyone gave. The Khazan–[[Xian Ti]] frontier has therefore run
  its own way for six hundred years, unaffected.
- **What one nation does inside its own borders.** [[Dalstan]] read this
  carefully and has been living in it ever since. Everything the Cathedral
  Court does to its own is outside the treaty; everything it does abroad is
  licensed, chartered, or a matter for the courts of the country it happens
  in. Nobody has yet worked out how to bring an inquisition before the Table.
- **Anything that is not a border.** Which is the whole of the current crisis.

## The towers break it

The Peace assumes the map is finished. Every mechanism in it — arbitration,
attendance, the credit clause — operates on claims to ground that existed when
the ground was surveyed and by parties who signed.

[[The Towers]] are none of that.

- **A tower is not a border dispute**, so the Table has no procedure for one,
  and the two sittings since the towers began have adjourned without a finding
  because the delegates could not agree what kind of thing they were arguing
  about.
- **Clause 2 does not bite on an expedition.** Thurion underwriting a chartered
  company walking into a door is not financing a war of aggression by any
  reading, and Thurion is underwriting a great many of them. For the first time
  in sixty years the Alliance's credit is flowing toward armed men with a
  competitive objective, and the clause that stopped the last war does not
  apply.
- **A conquered tower answers to nothing.** If the rumour is true — and it is
  believed almost universally — whoever comes out the master of a tower is
  rich beyond any crown's reckoning and beholden to no ledger. The Peace's
  entire power is that it can make a war unaffordable. It has no answer at all
  to a party that stops needing to be able to afford things.
- **Towers appear on ground nobody agreed to.** A border argument that has sat
  safely at the Table for thirty years converts, the morning a tower turns up
  in it, into a race with a prize at the end.

Nobody has said any of this at a Table sitting. Several delegations have said
it privately, and the [[Thurion Merchant Alliance]]'s own insurers have begun
writing memoranda that use the word *war* about the fourth or fifth year of
this, which no Thurion document has done in sixty years.

## At the table

- **This is why the world is peaceful and why that peace is brittle.** Players
  should be able to travel, trade and adventure across a continent that is
  genuinely not at war — and should be able to feel exactly which threads are
  load-bearing.
- **The Peace is an economic instrument, so it can be attacked economically.**
  Anyone who wanted a war would not need to raise an army. They would need
  Thurion's credit committee, or a Muster Court vote at Harrowgate, or a
  reason for one great house to stop appearing at Concord.
- **Nobody in the world is nostalgic for the war.** A villain arguing that the
  Peace has made everyone soft is arguing an unpopular position badly, and
  should be played as such. The real threat to it is not a warmonger. It is a
  door in a field that the treaty has no word for.
