Notes toward what conquering a tower could actually hand a party, beyond a
stat block and a treasure roll. None of this has been run at a table.
Nothing here has been checked against the rest of the vault for drift the
way a real note would be. Read it as a draft worth arguing with, not a rule
to enforce.

## The shape of it

Each tower is one bound sorcerer-king. See [[The Nameless Empire]] for
where that comes from. Before the ritual failed, each king kept six
apprentices: artificial congregations, individual people carrying the
weight of an entire population's forced devotion, meant to be spent in the
ritual's last step. The gods erased the apprentices' own engrams and let
them die. What survived is the empty machinery. Six vacant positions, and a
reservoir holding everyone who was sacrificed to fill them the first time.

A party that conquers a tower could put up to six of its own people into
those positions. Their souls and identities get recorded the same way the
apprentices' were. From then on, a killed body is a temporary problem. The
tower rebuilds it, spending one stored life to do it. Hundreds of thousands
of lives sit in a single reservoir, so running dry was never meant to be
the limit. The limit is what it costs to keep doing it.

## Numbers, if they hold

- **Twelve towers, ever.** The God Sundering's own account has the gods
  binding twelve kings. Taken literally, that's a hard ceiling on how many
  of these will ever exist, found or not.
- **Six seats each.** Untested against anything else in the vault. It's the
  number this idea started with.
- **Seventy-two Deathless, maximum, across the whole history of the
  world.** Follows from the two numbers above if both hold.

## Why the towers are only appearing now

The public position is that the towers are new, first seen in 2375 AR, and
[[The Towers]] is emphatic that the world genuinely believes that.

This idea's private version says otherwise. They aren't new. They were
always going to surface. Not stumbled into. Timed. The kings built a
fail-safe into their own ritual: if the ascension failed, resurface
eventually and let whoever's strong enough finish it by proxy. Whether
that's a fixed date the empire calculated in advance, or a condition the
world only just happened to meet, isn't settled. A fixed date is colder and
probably the better answer. It means the empire knew, twenty-three
centuries out, almost to the year, that this moment was coming.

## What conquering one gives

Two things, not one:

1. **A Deathless seat**, per the mechanic above.
2. **A second power, unique to that tower**, granted by the bound king and
   drawn from whatever they specialized in before the fall.

That second one isn't a guardian's reward for winning a fight. The king is
already bound to serve whoever proves strong enough to take them (see
[[The Nameless Empire]]), so the relationship keeps going past the moment
of conquest instead of ending there. What "proving strong enough" actually
requires, if not defeating the king outright, is still open. Two
candidates: a trial shaped by that king's own psychology, matching the
vault's existing "pitch is personality, not difficulty" line, or the
tower's own leftover defenses standing in for the fight the king itself
never offers.

## Level and pacing

Leaning toward **level 3–5**, deliberately early. The intended shape: a
still-weak party holding something every faction in the world would kill
for, and having to hide what they are instead of announcing it. That's a
different story than a capstone reward for a party that's already
powerful, and it needs most of a campaign left to run inside.

## The tower as home base

Pulled from [[The Nameless Empire]]'s own list of what the empire actually
built, rather than invented fresh: the portal network that reached
[[Luaris]], exosuits, railguns. A conquered tower's leftover infrastructure
is a plausible home base made entirely of things already on the books. The
"respec" idea fits the same way. Not a game concession, but the same
engram technology used to recalibrate a soul-pattern instead of restore
one. Nothing else in the setting could plausibly offer that.

## Death, still meaningful

Doomed is the mechanical backbone, playing off its actual rule. It lowers
the dying value that kills you, and normally clears with a full night's
rest.

- Every death adds a **permanent** stack of Doomed that does **not** clear
  on rest, overriding the normal behavior on purpose.
- It clears fully on **level-up**. Not a special ritual, just growth.
- Doomed 4 already means instant death on any drop to 0 HP, by the base
  rule. The open design question is what an uncleared fifth death does:
  nothing more, the effect just caps, or something worse, the engram
  itself starting to fray, up to a real risk of losing the character for
  good. Not decided.
- **Personality drift** rides alongside it, in the shape of Doctor Who
  regeneration. A small, GM-and-player-negotiated shift each time, nothing
  written down as a formal rule.

## Open questions

- Does the bound king serve the whole conquering party, or specifically the
  six who take a seat?
- Is the 2375 AR trigger a fixed date or a met condition?
- What happens past Doomed 4 with no level-up around to clear it?
- What does "proving strong enough" to bind a king look like at the table,
  if it isn't a straight fight?
- Do other parties' towers ever cross into this one's story, or do they
  stay background noise?
