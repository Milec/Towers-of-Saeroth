Every map still draws the Wildlands as one landmass, which is generous.
What anyone actually controls is a rim of coastline a few days deep, and
past it the maps just stop being maps and start being guesses.

- **Held ground** [[Kesmarch Frontier]], [[Thurion Merchant Alliance]] and
  [[Aquoniti]] each hold a single coastal enclave — Kesmarch for farmland
  it has run out of room for at home, the Alliance for a harbor it can
  chart a price on, Aquoniti for water it already knows how to sail. None
  of the three has ever agreed to call the others' claims a border, because
  none of them expects to still need one once the interior opens up.
- **The interior is ruins.** Broken stonework, streets under three feet of
  soil, walls too regular to be natural and too old for anyone to date —
  every survey that has gone more than a week inland has found more of it,
  and none has found where it ends.
- **Nothing has settled past the tree line, and it is not for lack of
  trying.** Every enclave has sent parties inland. Most come back short a
  few people and a firm opinion that going further was a mistake. Some do
  not come back at all.
- **One of them walked in.** Eleven years ago a woman came off an Alliance
  boat at the Aquoniti enclave, bought what she could carry, and asked three
  separate people for the shortest way inland. She would not say what she was
  after and did not appear to know. The harbour clerk's log calls her a
  pilgrim, which was a joke at the time and is the only word anyone has for
  it. She did not come back, which is not remarkable there. Kesmarch's rangers
  have a story about a man forty years ago that may be the same thing, and may
  be two men and a bad memory, and nobody has put the two accounts beside each
  other because they sit in different colonies' books.
- **What's out there does not have one name**, because no two colonies
  describe the same thing. Kesmarch's rangers call it "the wrong game" —
  tracks that belong to nothing, carcasses opened by nothing with teeth.
  Aquoniti's coastal watch describes shapes at the tree line that hold
  still exactly as long as they're being looked at. The Alliance simply
  stopped chartering ships past the second river. Nobody has compared notes
  carefully enough to notice how consistent the inconsistent reports are.

## The theories

None of it is confirmed, and no scholar has done the fieldwork to confirm
or deny any of it — the fieldwork is the part that doesn't come back.

- **The oldest guess** is that the ruins are simply older than anyone's
  written history, and that whoever built them is long dead of ordinary
  causes. This is the theory colonial charters prefer, because it is the
  one that makes the interior eventually safe to sell.
- **The theory that's gaining ground** is that something out there is
  still maintained — that the things in the interior behave less like
  wildlife and more like they're doing a job nobody remembers assigning
  them.
- **[[Aquoniti]] has said the least of the three, and has said it the most
  carefully.** Whatever its survey captains have logged along that coast
  hasn't made it into any report the other two colonies have seen.
