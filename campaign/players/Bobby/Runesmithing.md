---
title: Runesmithing
type: reference
who: Stoneborn Holds, first; Melisor Magocracy, a distant second
---

A rune is a symbol that means what it says, cut into something so it never
stops meaning it. A runesmith carves, brands, etches or paints one directly
onto a weapon, a wall, a door, a suit of armor, and the object simply
becomes what the rune describes. No gem to charge it, no circuit to carry
it, no season's wait for a working to run dry. Cut correctly, a rune lasts
as long as the metal it sits in.

## Against magitech

[[Magitech]] routes a working through a stone and a housing built to hold
it. Runesmithing skips both. The rune goes straight into the object it
changes, and once it's cut, it draws on nothing external to keep working —
not a charge, not a caster, not upkeep of any kind. That's also the whole
limitation. A magitech device can be wired into something larger. A rune
cannot. Whatever it does, it does to the one thing it was cut into, and
nothing else.

The two trades share a word for a reason nobody in either has confirmed.
Melisor's charge and property runes came out of Imperial ruins twenty years
ago, and the academies that recovered them have never once tested whether
the grammar underneath is the same one Stoneborn smiths have cut by hand
for centuries. Neither side has asked. Nobody profits from being the trade
that turns out to be the poor copy.

## Who's actually good at it

**Stoneborn Holds**, first, by a distance nobody argues with. The Holds
already measure a life's work by whether it outlasts the one who made it,
and a rune is the plainest test of that a smith ever faces. The oldest
runeblades in Highforge are still being carried, sharpened, and eventually
inherited, centuries after the hand that cut them died. Nobody outside the
Holds has matched the vocabulary a Stoneborn master can call on, or the
certainty that a cut, once made, will hold.

**Melisor Magocracy**, second, and not close. What Melisor has is theory,
not a tradition: the same grammar that lets its academies read a magitech
property rune and classify it also lets a Melisor-trained runesmith cut a
working one, on purpose, without ever having stood at a forge. It's a
colder mastery than the Holds', arrived at through study instead of
inheritance, and every Stoneborn smith who's met a Melisor-trained rival
agrees on that much even when they agree on nothing else.

Past those two, competence drops off fast. A few independent smiths know
the trick of a rune or two. Nobody else has the vocabulary.

## The cost

A rune cut wrong doesn't fail quietly. The object either does nothing at
all, or does something close enough to the intent to be dangerous — a ward
that blocks the wrong things, an edge that bites the hand as readily as the
target. A flawed cut can't be corrected. The metal gets melted down, or the
timber burned, and the smith starts over. That's why a masterwork rune
costs what it costs, and why a Stoneborn smith's name on a blade is worth
more than the steel under it.
