# Detecting Creatures  *Rules*

**Source** [[Sources/Core Rulebook]] pg. 465

There are three conditions that measure the degree to which you can sense a creature: [[Rules/Conditions/Observed|observed]], [[Rules/Conditions/Hidden|hidden]], and [[Rules/Conditions/Undetected|undetected]]. However, the [[Rules/Conditions/Concealed|concealed]] and [[Rules/Conditions/Invisible|invisible]] conditions can partially mask a creature, and the [[Rules/Conditions/Unnoticed|unnoticed]] condition indicates you have no idea a creature is around. In addition to the descriptions here, you can find these conditions in the Conditions Appendix on pages 618–623.

 With the exception of invisible, these conditions are relative to the viewer—it's possible for a creature to be observed to you but hidden from your ally. When you're trying to target a creature that's hard to see or otherwise sense, various drawbacks apply. Most of these rules apply to objects you're trying to detect as well as creatures.

 Typically, the GM tracks how well creatures detect each other, since neither party has perfect information. For example, you might think a creature is in the last place you sensed it, but it was able to [[Actions/Sneak]] away. Or you might think a creature can't see you in the dark, but it has [[Rules/Darkvision and Greater Darkvision|darkvision]].

 You can attempt to avoid detection by using the [[Rules/Sidebars/Being Stealthy|Stealth]] skill to [[Actions/Avoid Notice]], [[Actions/Hide]], or Sneak, or by using [[Rules/Skills/Deception]] to [[Actions/Create a Diversion]].
