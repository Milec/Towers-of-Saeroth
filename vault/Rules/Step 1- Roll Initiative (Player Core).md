# Step 1: Roll Initiative  *Rules*

**Source** [[Sources/Player Core]] pg. 435

When the GM calls for it, you'll roll initiative to determine your place in the initiative order, which is the sequence in which the encounter's participants will take their turns. Rolling initiative marks the start of an encounter. More often than not, you'll roll initiative when you enter a battle.

Typically, you'll roll a [[Rules/Perception (rules-2298)|Perception]] check to determine your initiative—the more aware you are of your surroundings, the more quickly you can respond. Sometimes, though, the GM might call on you to roll some other type of check. For instance, if you were [[Actions/Avoid Notice (Player Core)|Avoiding Notice]] during [[Rules/Exploration Mode (Player Core)|exploration]], you'd roll a [[Rules/Sidebars/Being Stealthy (Player Core)|Stealth]] check. A social encounter could call for a [[Rules/Skills/Deception (Player Core)|Deception]] or [[Rules/Skills/Diplomacy (Player Core)|Diplomacy]] check. In most cases, you can still use Perception if you prefer.

The GM rolls initiative for anyone other than the player characters in the encounter. If these include a number of identical creatures, the GM could roll once for the group as a whole and have them take their turns within the group in any order. However, this can make battles less predictable and more dangerous, so the GM might want to roll initiative for some or all creatures individually unless it's too much of a burden.

Unlike a check, where the result is compared to a DC, the results of initiative rolls are ranked. This ranking sets the order in which the encounter's participants act—the initiative order. The character with the highest result goes first. The second highest follows, and so on until whoever had the lowest result takes their turn last.

If your result is tied with an enemy's result, the enemy goes first. If your result is tied with another PC's, you can decide between yourselves who goes first when you reach that place in the initiative order. After that, your places in the initiative order usually don't change during the encounter. (But see the [[Actions/Delay (Player Core)|Delay]] basic action.)
