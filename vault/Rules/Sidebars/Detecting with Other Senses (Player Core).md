# Detecting with Other Senses  *Sidebar*

**Source** [[Sources/Player Core]] pg. 432

**Parent page** [[Rules/Senses (Player Core)|Senses]]

---

Most abilities that designate “a creature you can see” or the like function just as well if the user can precisely sense the subject with a different sense. If a monster uses a sense other than vision, the GM can adapt ways of avoiding detection that work with the monster's senses. For example, a creature that has echolocation might use hearing as a primary sense. This could mean its quarry is [[Rules/Concealed (Player Core)|concealed]] in a noisy chamber, [[Rules/Hidden (Player Core)|hidden]] in a great enough din, or [[Rules/Invisible (Player Core)|invisible]] under a [[Spells/Silence (Player Core)|_silence_]] spell.  

## Using Stealth With Other Senses

The [[Rules/Sidebars/Being Stealthy (Player Core)|Stealth]] skill is designed to use [[Actions/Hide (Player Core)|Hide]] for avoiding visual detection and [[Actions/Avoid Notice (Player Core)|Avoid Notice]] and [[Actions/Sneak (Player Core)|Sneak]] to avoid being both seen and heard. For many special senses, a player can describe how they're avoiding detection by that special sense and use the most applicable Stealth action. For instance, a creature stepping lightly to avoid being detected via [[Rules/Tremorsense (Player Core)|tremorsense]] would be using Sneak.  
In some cases, rolling a Dexterity-based Stealth skill check to Sneak doesn't make the most sense. For example, a PC trying to avoid being detected by a creature that senses heartbeats might meditate to slow their heart rate, using Wisdom instead of Dexterity for their Stealth check. When a creature could detect you using multiple different senses, use your lowest applicable attribute modifier.
