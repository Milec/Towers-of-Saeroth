# Hamatula (Barbed Devil)

Hamatulas are forged in Erebus to protect infernal vaults from those foolish enough to try to rob the archdevil [[Setting/Deities/Mammon]]. In case the vicious spines protruding from their bodies and ability to teleport weren't deadly enough, they are also clever wielders of warding glyphs. They are effective conjured guardians, though they resent being pulled away from their duties in [[Setting/Planes/Hell]]. Unlike most fiends, hamatulas are formed from several souls that lack any features distinctive enough to merit another role.

**Bloodletting**   On a critical hit, the hamatula's barbs deal 3d6 [[Rules/Sidebars/Persistent Damage Rules|persistent bleed damage]].

**Frightful Strike** free action ([[Traits/Divine]], [[Traits/Emotion]], [[Traits/Enchantment]], [[Traits/Fear]], [[Traits/Mental]]) **Trigger** The hamatula hits a creature with a barb Strike; **Effect** The creature struck must succeed at a DC 27 Will save or become [[Rules/Conditions/Frightened|frightened 2]] (or frightened 3 on a critical failure). Regardless of the result of its saving throw, the creature is then temporarily immune to Frightful Strike for 24 hours.

**Impaling Barb** two actions  The hamatula makes a barb Strike, then Strides up to half its Speed without triggering reactions. If the Strike hits, the hamatula impales the target with one of its barbs and snaps the barb free as it moves away from the target. This deals an additional 2d8 piercing damage to the target and pins it to an adjacent surface, rendering it [[Rules/Conditions/Immobilized|immobilized]] ([[Actions/Escape]] DC 29).

**Warden of Erebus**   A hamatula's [[Spells/Glyph of Warding|_glyph of warding_]] innate spell can contain any common spell from the Core Rulebook that meets the criteria in glyph of warding; the hamatula doesn't need to provide the spell.

> [!info] Sidebar
> ## Hamatula Treasures
>
>
>
> Perhaps because hamatulas exist to guard the riches of archdevils, infernal dukes, and queens of the night, they find themselves drawn to wealth. Though they despise summoners who pull them away from their assigned duties, hamatulas use their servitude as an opportunity to collect treasures and wealth that they can offer to their lords upon their return. Treasures that are painful or dangerous to handle are among their favorite prizes.
