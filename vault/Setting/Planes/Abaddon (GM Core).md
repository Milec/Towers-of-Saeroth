# Abaddon  *Plane*

**Source** [[Sources/GM Core]] pg. 179

**Category** Outer Sphere Planes

**Divinities** [[Setting/Deities/Ahriman]], [[Bestiary/Families/Daemon (Monster Core)|daemon harbingers]], [[Setting/Deities/Fumeiyoshi (Divine Mysteries Web Supplement)|Fumeiyoshi]], [[Setting/Deity Categories/Horsemen|Horsemen of the Apocalypse]], [[Setting/Deities/Lao Shu Po (Divine Mysteries Web Supplement)|Lao Shu Po]], [[Setting/Deities/Urgathoa (Divine Mysteries)|Urgathoa]], [[Setting/Deities/Zyphus]]

**Native Inhabitants** [[Bestiary/Families/Daemon (Monster Core)|daemons]], shades (the hunted)

---

A perpetual eclipse looms above the bleak wastelands of Abaddon, shedding an eerie half-light over a landscape of toxic, disease-ridden swamps, volcanic wastes, fog-shrouded forests, and the glittering, memory-devouring ribbon of the River Styx. An unnatural silence blankets the plane, cut only by the wails of shades falling from the sky like screaming, falling stars, or those already condemned upon the ground, desperate to find safety that doesn’t exist. [[Bestiary/Families/Daemon (Monster Core)|Daemons]], the physical embodiments of meaningless death and oblivion, roam unchecked, owing allegiance only to the [[Setting/Deity Categories/Horsemen|Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse]]: [[Setting/Deities/Charon|Death]], [[Setting/Deities/Trelmarixian|Famine]], [[Setting/Deities/Apollyon|Pestilence]], and [[Setting/Deities/Szuriel|War]]. In the courts of the Horsemen and the neutral grounds of trade cities such as Awaiting-Consumption, the soul trade serves daemonic hunger and industrialized extinction. [[Bestiary/Families/Hag (Monster Core)|Hags]] and other creatures ply the trade or make their way along the margins of daemonic society, eager to avoid consumption themselves by the plane’s nihilistic masters.

[[Setting/Deities/Urgathoa (Divine Mysteries)|Urgathoa]] and [[Setting/Deities/Zyphus]] claim divine domains here, their shades granted freedom from predation, yet something far worse glares down upon these gods and the Horseman alike. Abaddon’s perpetual eclipse might be nothing less than the lidded, comatose eye of the Bound Prince, the First Horseman, betrayed and cast down by the Four, forgotten by the cosmos at large, but far too powerful for them to destroy—waiting, watching, and hungering.
